# Proposing changes

Start with an issue for a question or a substantial proposal, or open a focused pull request for an edit. Link the relevant source passage and explain:

- What problem or ambiguity you found, and who it affects.
- The current text, your proposed outcome or wording, and why it helps.
- The sections, definitions, scope tags, and control IDs affected, including related cross-references.
- Any evidence, examples, alternatives, or tradeoffs that would help review.

For a factual correction, cite a reliable source. For a transcription concern, identify the exact passage so it can be checked against the provenance record and original document. Do not silently change the source baseline. Record confirmed source corrections in [source coverage](../maintenance/source-coverage.md) and the [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md).

For a proposed framework change, explain any change in normative effect and label the proposal clearly. Requirements are normative; Rationale and Assessment are informative, while the definitions and boundary rule have normative effect (Section 4). A proposal does not become a framework requirement merely because it appears in an issue, pull request, or derived page.

Reviewers may ask for clarification, suggest a narrower change, or raise effects on other controls. Keep discussion on the issue or pull request where practical, and summarize the outcome there. Follow the [editorial guide](editorial-guide.md) and [contribution guide](../CONTRIBUTING.md) when preparing the edit.
